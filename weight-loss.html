    Pharmacological Therapies

    Currently Available Weight Loss Medications

    We prescribe FDA-approved and clinically proven medications tailored to your health profile.

    GLP-1 Receptor Agonist

    Semaglutide (Wegovy®, Ozempic®)

    Administered once weekly. Mimics the naturally occurring GLP-1 hormone to slow digestion, suppress hunger signals in the brain, and stabilize blood sugar levels. Studies demonstrate an average of 15% total body weight reduction.

    Dual GIP / GLP-1 Agonist

    Tirzepatide (Zepbound®, Mounjaro®)

    The newest dual-action receptor agonist activating both GIP and GLP-1 pathways for maximum appetite regulation and metabolic enhancement. Clinical trials demonstrate up to 20%+ body weight loss.

    Appetite Suppressant

    Phentermine (Adipex-P®)

    An established sympathomimetic medication that stimulates the central nervous system to decrease appetite, ideal for short-to-medium term jumpstarts in appropriate candidates.

    Combination Therapy

    Phentermine-Topiramate & Contrave®

    Synergistic dual-medication formulations combining appetite suppression with neurotransmitter reuptake inhibitors to target cravings, emotional eating, and satiety signals.

    Criteria for Weight Loss Medication Prescriptions

    Physician-prescribed weight loss medications are generally approved for adults who meet the following clinical criteria:

    • A Body Mass Index (BMI) of 30 or higher (classified as obesity), OR
    • A Body Mass Index (BMI) of 27 or higher with at least one weight-related condition (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, or sleep apnea).
    Patient Questions

    Frequently Asked Questions About Weight Loss Medications

    How effective are prescription weight loss medications?
    When combined with lifestyle adjustments and physician supervision, medications like Semaglutide and Tirzepatide produce average weight losses between 15% and 22% of total body weight over 6 to 12 months—far exceeding the results of diet and exercise alone.
    How do GLP-1 medications work in the body?
    GLP-1 receptor agonists mimic an endogenous hormone that targets the hunger centers in the brain, drastically reducing food cravings ("food noise") and delaying gastric emptying so you feel full and satisfied with much smaller portions.
    How long do I need to take weight loss medications?
    Because obesity is recognized as a chronic metabolic condition, many patients benefit from a structured initial weight-loss phase followed by a tailored maintenance protocol under Dr. Gillespie's guidance.
    What is the difference between OTC diet pills and prescription medications?
    Over-the-counter dietary supplements are unregulated and lack rigorous clinical efficacy data. Prescription weight loss medications undergo extensive FDA clinical trials proving their safety, precise dosing mechanisms, and significant metabolic efficacy.
